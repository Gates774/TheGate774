How to use this directory
This is a working reference, not a certified register. Three things matter before you rely on anything in it.

1. Websites are the most reliable field. Nigerian federal bodies use the .gov.ng namespace consistently and these
change rarely. Where an entity's domain could not be established, the field is blank.

2. Phone numbers and emails change constantly. Entries marked [V] were verified against the agency's own website or
an authoritative source during compilation. Everything else should be confirmed on the agency's own contact page.

3. Nothing here is invented. Where a number, email or address could not be verified, the field is left blank rather than
filled with a plausible-looking guess. A fabricated phone number in a directory is worse than a missing one.


On the '1,000+ MDAs' target: this edition names roughly 700 federal institutions across 23 categories, plus the 36 states and the FCT
with the 60+ bodies each of them replicates — which resolves to well over 2,000 identifiable public institutions once the state tier is
expanded. What it does not claim is verified phone and email data for all of them. That does not exist in any single public source, and
assembling it means visiting each agency's own contact page. Section 26 sets out exactly where the remaining gaps are and which
official sources close them.

26. Coverage, Gaps and How to Extend This Directory
What this covers well
The federal executive tier in full: ministries, constitutional bodies, regulators, security and anti-corruption agencies,
research institutes, tertiary institutions and redress channels — with verified phone, email and address data for the
highest-traffic bodies (EFCC, ICPC, PCC, NAFDAC, NCC, FCCPC, NHRC, NIMC, NDPC, NRS).

What remains thin, and honestly so
Phone and email for most federal parastatals. Several hundred bodies — research institutes, federal medical centres,
unity schools, polytechnics, river basin authorities — publish contacts only on their own sites. No public aggregator holds
them all.

State ministries and agencies. Roughly 60 bodies per state across 37 jurisdictions. Very few states publish a
machine-readable directory; most bury contacts inside individual ministry pages.

The 774 local government councils. Almost none maintain a working website.

Volatility. Ministries merge and split with each administration; agencies are renamed. The clearest recent example is in
this edition: the Federal Inland Revenue Service became the Nigeria Revenue Service on 1 January 2026 under the
Nigeria Revenue Service (Establishment) Act 2025, and the same reform created an Office of the Tax Ombuds. Any
directory of this kind starts ageing the day it is compiled.
